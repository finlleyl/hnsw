# G2: near-duplicates

Exact-bit merge keeps one node per distinct vector. This gate checks copies that differ
by a little noise, so they stay separate nodes under exact merge, and asks whether they
strand nodes the way exact copies did before merging.

**Setup.** darwin/arm64, Apple M4 Pro, go1.26.2. Clustered synthetic data (the generator
in `index_test.go`), d=128, M=16, EfConstruction 200, seeds 1, 2 and 3. Each run holds
20000 keys, and the first 1000 or 5000 are copies of one clustered vector. "1 ulp" moves
each nonzero element of a copy one ulp up or down at random. A jitter of a adds uniform
noise in [-a, a] to each element, and the copy is then normalized. "First" inserts the
copies before the other keys, "last" after them. Recall is tie-aware recall@10 at ef=100
over 1000 held-out queries, worst of the three seeds. Unreached counts live nodes that a
layer-0 walk from the entry misses, worst of the three seeds. The distance column is the
largest float32 distance from the first copy to the next 199.

## Result

Fail without τ, pass with it. Up to a jitter of 1e-4 the copies sit within float32
rounding of each other, their distances tie, and Algorithm 4 strands up to 4651 nodes.
Merging within τ = 1e-6 makes them one node, with 0 unreached and recall at least .9986
at every gate level (1 ulp, 1e-7, 1e-6, 1e-5). So the consequence is applied: a key
whose vector lies within τ of a stored one is served by the stored vector.

**Open, outside the gate.** At jitter 1e-3 and 1e-2 the copies are a tight cluster, not
rounding noise, and τ does not merge them. 5000 of them inserted first leave 6-10 nodes
unreached, and inserted last they cut recall to .9716. A related case came up while
writing the revive test: 5000 points of isotropic noise around a stored vector, at
distances up to about 0.2, left 3088-4618 of them unreached. The likely cause is that
the stored vector is nearer to each point than the points are to each other, so
Algorithm 4 prunes every link but the one to it.
Both are properties of Algorithm 4 on dense isotropic clusters, not of duplicate
handling, and neither occurs in the clustered data, whose clusters are low rank. Only
d=128 was measured; the float32 tie band is wider at higher d.
